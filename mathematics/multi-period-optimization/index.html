    Multi-Period Portfolio Optimization (MPO) — Mathematical Foundations¶

    Single-period portfolio optimization (SPO) treats each rebalance date \(t\) as an isolated static problem, ignoring the intertemporal coupling induced by transaction costs, nonlinear market impact, multi-horizon alpha decay, and future rebalancing flexibility.

    PyAlloq implements a unified 3-Pillar Multi-Period Portfolio Optimization (MPO) framework bridging:

    1. Classical Convex Model Predictive Control (MPC) & Operator Splitting (ADMM) (Boyd et al., 2017; Gârleanu & Pedersen, 2013)
    2. Decision-Focused Deep Learning (DFL) & Entropic Mirror-Descent Fixed-Point (MDFP) Layers (Agrawal et al., 2019; Elmachtoub & Grigas, 2022)
    3. FinRL Deep Reinforcement Learning, Two-Level Bayesian HRP (RL-BHRP), Deep Hedging Trajectory Optimization, & Thermodynamic Regime Gating (Buehler et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2022; Moroke et al., 2026)

    1. Intertemporal Wealth & Self-Financing Dynamics¶

    Let \(N\) risky assets (plus an optional cash account) have gross simple return vector \(R_{t+1} = \mathbf{1} + r_{t+1} \in \mathbb{R}_{++}^N\) over period \([t, t+1]\).

    At the beginning of period \(t\):

    • \(w_{t^-} \in \Delta^N\) denotes the pre-trade drifted weight vector inherited from period \(t-1\).
    • \(z_t \in \mathbb{R}^N\) denotes the normalized trade vector executed at time \(t\), resulting in the post-trade weight vector: [ w_t = w_{t^-} + z_t, \qquad \mathbf{1}^\top z_t + \phi_{\text{trade}}(z_t) = 0 \quad (\text{or } \mathbf{1}^\top w_t = 1 \text{ under self-financing rescale}). ]
    • Over period \([t, t+1]\), market returns cause the post-trade weights \(w_t\) to drift nonlinearly to the next pre-trade state \(w_{(t+1)^-}\): [ w_{(t+1)^-} = \frac{w_t \circ (\mathbf{1} + r_{t+1})}{1 + r_{p, t+1}^{\text{net}}}, \qquad r_{p, t+1}^{\text{net}} = (1 - \phi_{\text{trade}}(z_t))(1 + w_t^\top r_{t+1}) - 1. ]

    Within a convex lookahead planning horizon \(\tau = t, \dots, t+H-1\) where future returns are small (\(\|r_\tau\|_\infty \ll 1\)), the linearized intertemporal state transition holds: [ w_\tau = w_{\tau-1} + z_\tau, \qquad w_{t-1} \equiv w_{t^-}. ]

    2. Microstructural Transaction Cost & Market Impact Models¶

    At each planning stage \(\tau\), rebalancing trades \(z_\tau = w_\tau - w_{\tau-1}\) incur temporary and permanent execution costs:

    \[ \phi_{\text{trade}}(z_\tau) = \underbrace{\left(\kappa_{\text{spread}}^\top |z_\tau|\right)}_{\text{Proportional Bid-Ask } (p=1)} + \underbrace{\left(\kappa_{\text{impact}}^\top |z_\tau|^{3/2}\right)}_{\text{Square-Root Market Impact } (p=3/2)} + \underbrace{\frac{1}{2} z_\tau^\top \Lambda_\tau z_\tau}_{\text{Quadratic Temporary Cost } (p=2)}. \]

    2.1 Proportional Bid-Ask Spread (\(p=1\)) & the No-Trade Zone¶

    The \(\ell_1\) penalty \(\kappa_{\text{spread}}^\top |w_\tau - w_{\tau-1}|\) is non-differentiable at \(z_{\tau,i} = 0\). By first-order subdifferential KKT conditions: [ \hat{\mu}{\tau,i} - \gamma (\Sigma\tau w_\tau)i - \nu\tau \in [-\kappa_{\text{spread},i}, +\kappa_{\text{spread},i}] \implies z_{\tau,i}^ = 0. ] Thus, proportional costs create a rigorous polyhedral no-trade region* around \(w_{t^-}\): the optimizer holds existing positions untouched unless the marginal risk-adjusted alpha exceeds the half-spread threshold \(\kappa_{\text{spread},i}\).

    2.2 Square-Root Market Impact (\(p=3/2\)) via Power Cones¶

    Following Almgren & Chriss (2001) and Boyd et al. (2017), executing a fraction of Average Daily Volume (ADV) incurs price impact proportional to \(|z_{\tau,i}|^{1/2}\), yielding total dollar cost proportional to \(|z_{\tau,i}|^{3/2}\): [ \kappa_{\text{impact},i} |z_{\tau,i}|^{3/2}, \qquad \kappa_{\text{impact},i} \propto \sigma_i \sqrt{\frac{V_{\text{port}}}{\text{ADV}_i}}. ] In CVXPY, cp.power(cp.abs(z_tau), 1.5) is canonicalized into 3D power cones / second-order cones (SOCP) solvable in polynomial interior-point iterations.

    2.3 Gârleanu-Pedersen (2013) Permanent Price Impact & Aim Portfolios¶

    When large institutional trades move the equilibrium market price permanently, an intertemporal price-distortion state \(q_\tau \in \mathbb{R}^N\) evolves with resilience rate \(\rho_{\text{perm}} \in (0, 1]\): [ q_\tau = (1 - \rho_{\text{perm}}) q_{\tau-1} + \eta_{\text{perm}} z_\tau, \qquad q_{t-1} = \mathbf{0}, ] imposing an additional stage execution drag \(\frac{1}{2} q_\tau^\top q_\tau\). Under quadratic costs and exponential alpha decay, Gârleanu & Pedersen (2013) prove that the optimal multi-period policy trades partially at constant speed toward an Aim Portfolio \(w_t^{\text{aim}}\), which itself is a discounted convex combination of future single-period Markowitz portfolios.

    4. Pillar 1: Classical Convex MPC & ADMM Operator Splitting¶

    4.1 Deterministic Receding-Horizon MPC Formulation¶

    At each rebalance date \(t\), MultiPeriodMPCAllocator solves the \(H\)-stage convex optimization problem:

    \[ \begin{aligned} \max_{\{w_\tau, z_\tau\}_{\tau=t}^{t+H-1}} \quad & \sum_{\tau=t}^{t+H-1} \beta^{\tau-t} \left[ \hat{\mu}_{\tau|t}^\top w_\tau - \frac{\gamma}{2} w_\tau^\top \hat{\Sigma}_{\tau|t} w_\tau - \phi_{\text{trade}}(z_\tau) - \phi_{\text{hold}}(w_\tau) \right] - \frac{\gamma_{\text{term}}}{2} (w_{t+H-1} - w_{\text{bench}})^\top \hat{\Sigma}_{t+H-1|t} (w_{t+H-1} - w_{\text{bench}}) \\ \text{subject to} \quad & w_\tau = w_{\tau-1} + z_\tau, \qquad w_{t-1} = w_{t^-}, \\ & \mathbf{1}^\top w_\tau = 1, \qquad w_{\min} \le w_\tau \le w_{\max}, \\ & \|z_\tau\|_1 \le \tau_{\max} \quad \forall \tau \in \{t, \dots, t+H-1\}. \end{aligned} \]

    By the Receding-Horizon Principle, only the immediate stage decision \(w_t^*\) is executed in the market; at \(t+1\), the horizon rolls forward with updated pre-trade weights \(w_{(t+1)^-}\).

    4.2 High-Speed Operator Splitting via ADMM¶

    For large universes or high-frequency backtests where interior-point solvers become a bottleneck, MultiPeriodMPCAllocator(backend="admm") reformulates the coupled \(H\)-stage problem using Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM):

    1. Primal Weight & Trade Variable Split: Introduce auxiliary split variables \(u_\tau = z_\tau = w_\tau - w_{\tau-1}\) and \(v_\tau = w_\tau\) with scaled dual variables \(\eta_\tau, \xi_\tau\).
    2. Block-Tridiagonal KKT Linear System (\(w\)-update): Solving for the stacked trajectory \(W = (w_t, \dots, w_{t+H-1})\) reduces to a positive-definite block-tridiagonal linear system inverted via block Thomas elimination.
    3. Closed-Form Proximal Soft-Thresholding (\(u\)-update): The non-smooth \(\ell_1\) bid-ask cost and turnover ball decouple into elementwise soft-thresholding: [ \mathcal{S}_{\kappa / \rho}(x) = \operatorname{sign}(x) \max(|x| - \kappa/\rho, \, 0), ] followed by Euclidean projection onto the \(\ell_1\)-turnover ball.
    4. Capped Simplex Projection (\(v\)-update): Projects each \(v_\tau\) onto \(\{w \in \mathbb{R}^N : \mathbf{1}^\top w = 1, \, w_{\min} \le w \le w_{\max}\}\) in \(O(N \log N)\) time.

    5. Pillar 2: Decision-Focused Learning (DFL) & Differentiable MDFP Layers¶

    5.1 The Objective Mismatch of Two-Stage "Predict-Then-Optimize"¶

    Training a neural network exclusively on Mean Squared Error \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}(\hat{\mu}, y) = \|\hat{\mu} - y\|_2^2\) treats all return forecast errors equally. However, inside a constrained multi-period optimizer:

    • Errors on assets deep inside the no-trade zone have zero impact on portfolio weights.
    • Small sign errors on assets near the simplex boundary or with low covariance can trigger massive, costly turnover cascades across stages \(t, \dots, t+H-1\).

    5.2 Entropic Mirror-Descent Fixed-Point (MDFP) Optimization Layer¶

    To embed multi-period optimization directly inside PyTorch computation graphs without expensive CPU-bound conic solvers, MDFPOptimizationLayer solves the coupled \(H\)-stage simplex problem using Entropic Mirror Descent (Exponentiated Gradient) under the Kullback-Leibler Bregman divergence \(D_{\text{KL}}(z \| z')\):

    [ z_{s,i}^{(m+1)} = \frac{z_{s,i}^{(m)} \exp!\left(-\eta \, g_{s,i}^{(m)}\right)}{\sum_{j=1}^N z_{s,j}^{(m)} \exp!\left(-\eta \, g_{s,j}^{(m)}\right)} \equiv \mathcal{F}_{s,i}!\left(z^{(m)}, \theta\right), ] where \(g_s^{(m)} = \nabla_{z_s} J(z^{(m)}; \theta)\) is the stage gradient incorporating expected returns, covariance risk \(\gamma \Sigma_s z_s\), and smoothed inter-stage turnover coupling \(\kappa \frac{z_s - z_{s-1}}{\sqrt{(z_s - z_{s-1})^2 + \epsilon^2}}\).

    5.3 \(O(1)\)-Memory Implicit Fixed-Point Differentiation¶

    Rather than unrolling \(M\) mirror-descent steps in autograd memory, MDFPOptimizationLayer differentiates through the converged fixed point \(z^* = \mathcal{F}(z^*, \theta)\) via the Implicit Function Theorem: [ z^(\theta) = \mathcal{F}(z^(\theta), \theta) \implies \left(I - \frac{\partial \mathcal{F}}{\partial z^}\right) \frac{\partial z^}{\partial \theta} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{F}}{\partial \theta}. ] In the backward pass, given upstream gradient \(v = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z^*}\), we solve the adjoint linear system via a damped Neumann Series of Vector-Jacobian Products (VJPs) evaluated exclusively at \(z^*\): [ u^\top = v^\top \left(I - \lambda \frac{\partial \mathcal{F}}{\partial z^}\right)^{-1} = \sum_{k=0}^K v^\top \left(\lambda \frac{\partial \mathcal{F}}{\partial z^}\right)^k, \qquad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta} = u^\top \frac{\partial \mathcal{F}(z^*, \theta)}{\partial \theta}. ]

    6. Pillar 3: FinRL DRL, RL-BHRP, Deep Hedging & Thermodynamic Gates¶

    6.1 FinRL Markov Decision Process (MDP) & Turbulence Circuit Breaker¶

    In PortfolioGymEnv, multi-period portfolio management is cast as a continuous-control MDP \((\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{P}, \mathcal{R}, \gamma)\):

    • State \(s_t\): Concatenation of pre-trade drifted weights \(w_{t^-}\), lookback return tensor \(R_{t-L+1:t}\), rolling volatilities \(\sigma_t\), Mahalanobis Turbulence Index \(d_t\), and Bures-Wasserstein regime distance \(W_2\): [ d_t = \frac{1}{N} (r_t - \hat{\mu}_t)^\top \hat{\Sigma}_t^{-1} (r_t - \hat{\mu}_t). ]
    • Reward \(r_t\): Differential log net wealth growth penalized by quadratic return variance and thermodynamic turnover friction: [ r_t = \ln(1 + r_{p,t}^{\text{net}}) - \frac{\gamma}{2} (r_{p,t}^{\text{net}})^2 - \eta_{W_2} W_2(t) |w_t - w_{t^-}|_1. ]

    6.2 Two-Level Bayesian Hierarchical Risk Parity (RL-BHRP)¶

    BayesianHRPAllocator resolves the return-insensitivity of classical HRP without inverting ill-conditioned \(N \times N\) covariance matrices:

    1. Intra-Cluster Level: Hierarchical clustering partitions the \(N\) assets into \(K\) correlation clusters, and recursive bisection computes risk-parity intra-cluster weights \(W_{\text{intra}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times K}\).
    2. Inter-Cluster Bayesian Level: Cluster-level equilibrium implied returns \(\pi_C = \Sigma_C b_{\text{hrp}}\) are blended with sample/predicted cluster views \(\mu_C^{\text{view}}\): [ \bar{\mu}_C = (1 - \tau) \pi_C + \tau \mu_C^{\text{view}}, ] and cluster budgets are tilted toward high-posterior-Sharpe clusters while damping cluster turnover against \(w_{t^-}\).
